VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  => INTERVAL-LEVEL RESTRICTION IS THE CULPRIT.

     On blocks scored identically by both arms -- where block-level
     gating makes no difference by construction -- the gated pipeline is
     still 0.718 bpm worse. The harm therefore comes from
     restricting J-peak detection to gate-accepted seconds INSIDE
     retained blocks, not from rejecting whole blocks.

     Mechanism: stripping ~28% of seconds fragments the J-J interval
     sequence, leaving fewer and more scattered intervals to average,
     so the block HR estimate gets noisier even though the seconds
     removed were individually lower-quality.

     ACTIONABLE: test a BLOCK-GATING-ONLY variant -- use the gate to
     accept/reject whole blocks, but run J-peak detection over the
     entire retained block. That keeps the quality gate's purpose while
     removing the mechanism that hurts. If that variant beats ungated,
     you have a positive result AND an explanation for the negative one.

  NOTE ON beta=0.90: its apparently low MAE in patch1 is NOT gate value.
  At 3.3% coverage the arms score entirely different block subsets, so that
  comparison is selection, not performance. Only the common-block numbers
  above support a like-for-like claim.
